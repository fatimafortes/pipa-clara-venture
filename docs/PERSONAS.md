# Pipa Clara — Customer Personas

**Version:** v1 (Week 12). Built from my User research (Week 11 brief and the conversation with a real organizer, last delivery October 14, 2026).
Names and identifying details are **invented**. These personas are composites, not real people. They are who my persona tests impersonate and who my marketing targets; they get updated when real users correct them.

---

## Persona 1: Rosa, the organizer (primary user)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 55 |
| **Context** | Lives in a vecindad where about 12 households share one cistern. When the water runs low, she is the one who calls the water company, waits for the truck, pays the driver in full at the door (cash or transfer) and then collects each family's share. She keeps the record herself, in her head, on paper or in WhatsApp messages. Uses WhatsApp every day; other apps only if they are simple. |
| **The need, in her words** | *"Cuando faltó agua, los vecinos empezaron a decir que unos gastaban más que otros, y yo no tenía cómo demostrar nada. Le dije al pipero y dijo que iba a hacer un reporte, y nunca pasó nada."* ("When we ran short, the neighbors started saying some households used more than others, and I had no way to prove anything. I told the driver, he said he'd file a report, and nothing ever happened.") |
| **What she does today** | Trusts the number the driver says. Splits the cost by memory or in the WhatsApp group. When a load seems short, the argument lands between neighbors; a complaint to the company goes nowhere because there is no evidence. Pays at delivery and refuses to pay in advance because she doesn't want to take that risk. |
| **What she'd pay** | Nothing out of her own pocket. She'd accept a small fee added to each household's share: about MXN 2 per household per delivery to start, up to about MXN 4 once she sees it works. |
| **What would make her quit** | Measuring takes too long while the driver is waiting and in a hurry. The driver gets annoyed and the relationship sours. The app is hard to understand at the first try. The neighbors don't believe the summary anyway, so she is still the one taking the blame. |

## Persona 2: Claudia, the skeptical neighbor (secondary user, pays her share)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 41 |
| **Context** | Lives in the same vecindad as Rosa, works outside the home most of the day, two kids. Never orders the truck and never opens the app. Receives the summary in the WhatsApp group and pays her share to Rosa by transfer. Pays the Pipa Clara fee as part of that share, so she is a paying customer even though she never logs in. |
| **The need, in her words** | *"¿Y yo cómo sé que de verdad llegaron diez mil litros? Yo casi ni estoy en la casa y siempre me toca lo mismo."* ("How do I know ten thousand liters really arrived? I'm hardly ever home and I always pay the same.") |
| **What she does today** | Pays what Rosa tells her, with a quiet doubt. When the water runs out early she suspects the driver, Rosa, or the neighbors who are home all day, and has no way to tell which. Sometimes delays paying until someone explains. |
| **What she'd pay** | Her MXN 2–4 share of the fee, only if the summary shows her something she couldn't check before: the liters measured against the liters claimed, and a photo of the "before" reading taken with the driver present. |
| **What would make her quit** | The summary feels like Rosa's word in a nicer format, with no proof she can see. A fee shows up in her share without explanation. Her name and payment status are exposed to the whole group. A "short" verdict the driver disputes, with nobody able to tell who is right. |
